## §1. 導入と問い　⏱ 1:30　🖼 Slide 1–2

🎯 **残す一文**: 「国内の移転が削られている最中に外部移転が来ると何が起きるか」

> Thank you, Chair. My name is Shin Suzuki, from the Faculty of Economics at Fukuyama University, Japan. I'm glad to be in this stream, because my paper treats regional inequality as, above all, **a fiscal question**.
>
> Here is the starting point. Whether residents of a remote municipality in Lapland or Kainuu receive health care, social services and schooling comparable to Helsinki is **not settled by national social legislation alone**. In a service welfare state like Finland, where municipalities carry primary responsibility for provision, it depends on the architecture that **finances** municipal service delivery: the local tax base, the state grants and equalisation that compensate for territorial differences, and the regional policy that channels development resources to the periphery. **Universalism, in this sense, is a territorial achievement.**
>
> Now, Finland reorganised **every element** of that architecture within a single decade. A deep crisis and fiscal consolidation forced cuts in state grants. A reform in 1993 replaced cost-based matching grants with general grants. And accession to the EU in 1995 inserted an entirely new layer of territorial finance — the Structural Funds, including **Objective 6**, created in the accession negotiations for the sparsely populated areas of Finland and Sweden.
>
> So the puzzle is **not** whether peripheral municipalities received EU money. They did. The puzzle is: **what happens to the fiscal foundations of territorial service delivery when an external, project-based transfer arrives precisely as the domestic, formula-based transfer system is being cut and redesigned?** Both processes acted on the same municipalities, **in opposite directions**, administered by different ministries under different policy logics.
>
> That is my question: **how did EU transfers, national grants and equalisation, and municipal finance interact in Finland's peripheral regions after accession — and what does that interaction imply for the fiscal conditions of territorial service delivery?**

## §2. 先行研究のギャップ　⏱ 1:00　🖼 Slide 3

🎯 **残す一文**: 追加性研究は国内ベースラインを所与にし、リスケーリング研究は政府間財政に踏み込んでいない

> Existing research does not resolve this. The **additionality literature** asks whether EU funds add to or replace national expenditure, and finds substitution in several member states. It has taught us a great deal — but it works with **aggregate public expenditure**, and it tends to leave **the domestic institutional baseline unexamined**.
>
> Work on **cohesion policy governance** describes multilevel institutions without tracing how transfers are **absorbed into subnational budgets**; and the large literature measuring the **economic effects** of the funds addresses outcomes rather than the fiscal channel through which the money passes.
>
> The **welfare state rescaling literature**, in turn, has established that social policy is territorially mediated — but it has engaged only lightly with the grant and equalisation systems that make territorial provision **fiscally possible**. That is the terrain on which fiscal federalism has long shown that grant effects are not straightforward.
>
> My paper sits at that intersection: **an external transfer meeting a domestic grant system that is contracting at the same moment.**

**日本語訳**：既存研究はこれに答えていません。**追加性文献**はEU資金が国内支出への上乗せか置換かを問い、複数の加盟国で代替の証拠を見出しています。多くを教えてくれますが、扱うのは**集計的な公的支出**であり、**国内の制度的ベースラインは未検討のまま**にする傾向があります。／**結束政策のガバナンス研究**は多層的制度を記述しますが、移転が**下位政府の予算にどう吸収されるか**は追いません。基金の**経済効果**を測る大きな文献も、資金が通る財政チャネルではなく成果を扱います。／**福祉国家リスケーリング文献**は、社会政策が領域的に媒介されることを確立しましたが、領域的供給を**財政的に可能にする**補助金・財政調整の制度への関与は薄い。そこは財政連邦主義が長年「補助金の効果は単純ではない」と示してきた地形です。／本稿はその交差点に立ちます：**同じ時点で収縮している国内補助金制度に、外部移転が出会う場面です。**

## §3. 概念：pseudo-complementarity の3次元　⏱ 2:00　🖼 Slide 4（表1）

🎯 **残す一文**: 3次元に分けること自体が貢献。強い意味では3つすべてが要る

> Let me introduce the concept the paper works with. I use **pseudo-complementarity** to describe a configuration in which EU transfers **formally satisfy** the principles of complementarity and additionality, **while the fiscal and governance context in which they land changes in ways those principles do not capture.**
>
> The term has been used loosely in earlier work. What I try to do is **separate it into three dimensions**, each with its own mechanism and its own evidence base.
>
> **First, institutional complementarity**: the formal design of the EU layer as supplementary finance — a distinct objective, its own eligibility rules, co-financing requirements and partnership bodies, legally additional to national expenditure. This is directly observable in documents.
>
> **Second, fiscal substitution**: a decline in the domestic resource baseline coinciding with the arrival of EU funds, such that the combined public flow to assisted areas grows **less than the EU contribution implies**. Note that substitution may occur at **more than one scale** — within a municipal budget line, within a ministry's programme budget, or within the aggregate state budget — and **the scale matters for whether it is observable at all**.
>
> **Third, administrative transformation**: a shift in the **governance form** of territorial finance — from entitlement-based, formula-allocated general grants towards competitive, time-limited, co-financed project funding.
>
> 🔻 Two points about falsification, because I think this is where the concept earns its keep. **The dimensions are separable**: institutional complementarity and administrative transformation can hold **without** fiscal substitution — in which case the honest description is *a change in the form of territorial finance*, not pseudo-complementarity. And **evidence against fiscal substitution** would include adjusted estimates that reverse the unadjusted pattern. As you'll see, **that is partly the case here** — so I treat that dimension as **unresolved rather than confirmed**.
>
> **Pseudo-complementarity in the strong sense requires all three. In this case, two hold and one does not.**

**日本語訳**：本稿が用いる概念を紹介します。**疑似補完性**とは、EU移転が補完性・追加性の原則を**形式的には満たしている**一方で、**それが着地する財政・ガバナンスの文脈が、それらの原則では捉えられない仕方で変化している**配置を指します。／この語は先行研究では緩やかに使われてきました。本稿の試みは、**3つの次元に分離する**ことです。／**第一に制度的補完性**：EU層が補完的財源として形式設計されていること——固有の目標、独自の適格性規則、協調融資要件、パートナーシップ機関、そして国内支出に法的に追加的であること。文書で直接観察できます。／**第二に財政的代替**：EU資金の到着と同時に国内の資源ベースラインが低下し、被支援地域への公的資金の合計が**EUの寄与が含意するより少なくしか増えない**こと。代替は**複数のスケール**で起こりうる——自治体の予算項目内、省のプログラム予算内、国家予算全体——そして**どのスケールかが、そもそも観察可能かどうかを左右します**。／**第三に行政的変容**：領域財政の**ガバナンス形式**の転換——権利ベース・定式配分の一般財源型補助金から、競争的・期限付き・協調融資のプロジェクト資金へ。／反証可能性について2点。**3次元は分離可能**です。①と③は②なしに成立しうる。その場合の誠実な記述は「疑似補完性」ではなく**「領域財政の形式変化」**です。そして**②に反する証拠**には、調整推定が非調整のパターンを反転させることが含まれます。ご覧のとおり**本稿では部分的にそれが起きている**ので、この次元は**確認ではなく未決**として扱います。／**強い意味での疑似補完性は3つすべてを要求します。本事例では2つが成立し、1つは成立しません。**

## §4. データと方法（限界を先に言う）　⏱ 1:30　🖼 Slide 5

🎯 **残す一文**: 記述的である。因果効果は同定しない。それを先に言う

> The evidence is of two kinds. **Process tracing** of the creation and domestic implementation of Objective 6 and of the domestic grant reform, using the Accession Treaty and Protocol No. 6, the Structural Funds regulation, Finnish administrative and audit materials, and contemporary press coverage. And a **municipality-level panel** I constructed from Statistics Finland's historical municipal finance statistics.
>
> The main panel covers **452 municipalities, 52 accounting functions, and the years 1997 to 1999** — just over seventy thousand municipality-by-function-by-year cells. It records subsidies booked as received "**from the European Union**", state sectoral subsidies, and expenditure by function. A second, shorter dataset covers **total state grants and their four components for 1993 to 1996**, under the pre-1997 accounting regime. **The two belong to different accounting regimes and do not form a continuous panel**, so I analyse changes *within* each period.
>
> Amounts are estimated with **Poisson pseudo-maximum-likelihood, with population as an exposure offset**, so coefficients read as rate ratios per resident; PPML because the outcome contains **a large share of exact zeros**. Standard errors are clustered at municipality level, and where several function groups are tested simultaneously, p-values are **FDR-adjusted**.
>
> Now let me state the limits **before** the results rather than after them, because they shape how the results should be read.
>
> **First: none of these estimates identifies a causal effect.** Objective 6 status is not randomly assigned — it correlates with geography, industrial structure and demography — the panel is short, and it spans an accounting regime change. **Several key estimates are imprecise, and I report them as such throughout.**
>
> **Second: the accounting item "from the European Union" is not identical to the Structural Funds.** It records what municipalities *booked* as EU-sourced. It is an indicator of the EU layer **as it reached municipal ledgers**, not a measure of Structural Fund disbursement.
>
> **Third: the municipal ledger does not capture joint municipal authorities** — which produce much specialised health care and vocational education — nor regional councils, enterprises or associations. Substantial parts of both the welfare system and the Structural Fund programmes are **outside the data by construction**.
>
> **And fourth: the paper measures fiscal flows, not welfare outcomes.** It observes no service access, quality, staffing or coverage. Its claims concern **the fiscal and institutional conditions surrounding** municipal welfare provision — not provision itself.

**日本語訳**：証拠は2種類です。**プロセス追跡**——目標6の創設と国内実施、国内補助金改革を、加盟条約と議定書6、構造基金規則、フィンランドの行政・監査資料、当時の報道から追います。そして**自治体レベルのパネル**——フィンランド統計局の歴史的自治体財政統計から著者が構築しました。／主パネルは**452自治体、52会計機能、1997–99年**——7万強のセル。「**EUから**」受領と計上された補助金、国庫の部門別補助金、機能別支出を記録します。副データは**1993–96年の国庫支出金総額と4構成要素**（1997年前の会計制度）。**両者は会計制度が異なり連続パネルを構成しない**ので、各期間**内**の変化を分析します。／金額は**人口をエクスポージャー・オフセットに入れたPPML**で推定し、係数は住民1人当たりの率比として読めます。PPMLを使うのは、被説明変数に**厳密なゼロが大量にある**からです。標準誤差は自治体クラスター、複数機能群の同時検定では**FDR調整**を行います。／ここで限界を、結果の**後ではなく前に**述べます。結果の読み方を規定するからです。／**第一に、いずれの推定も因果効果を同定しません。** 目標6ステータスはランダム割当ではなく（地理・産業構造・人口と相関）、パネルは短く、会計制度変更をまたぎます。**主要な推定のいくつかは不精確で、本稿はそれを一貫してそのように報告します。**／**第二に、会計項目「EUから」は構造基金と同一ではありません。** 自治体がEU由来と**計上した**ものを記録するにすぎず、**自治体台帳に届いた限りでの**EU層の指標であって、構造基金支出の計測ではありません。／**第三に、自治体台帳は自治体連合を捕捉しません**——専門医療や職業教育の多くを担っているにもかかわらず——地域議会、企業、団体も同様です。福祉システムと構造基金プログラムの相当部分が**構成上データの外**にあります。／**第四に、本稿が測るのは財政フローであって福祉アウトカムではありません。** サービスのアクセス・質・人員・カバレッジは観察しません。主張は自治体福祉供給**を取り巻く財政的・制度的条件**についてであり、供給そのものについてではありません。

## §5. 歴史①：目標6の創設　⏱ 1:30　🖼 Slide 6　🔻短縮可

🎯 **残す一文**: 受給要件が「経済的後進性」から「恒久的な人口希薄性」へ移り、その地理は伝統的支援地域と一致した

> Very briefly on how the EU layer was built, because its construction matters for how we should read it.
>
> Two shocks arrived at once. **The Soviet collapse of 1991** made Western integration a security priority: Finland applied in March 1992. And **the economy contracted sharply** — Soviet trade disappeared, a credit boom ended, unemployment exceeded **eighteen per cent** by 1994. Facing large deficits, cuts to state grants entered the political agenda.
>
> The decisive obstacle was **agriculture**. The farmers' union MTK — the core constituency of Prime Minister Aho's Centre Party — was strongly opposed, and a 1992 report projected that accepting the CAP without special measures would **halve producer prices** and cut production by some **eighty per cent**. **Agricultural and rural compensation thus became a political condition of accession itself.**
>
> The breakthrough came at the turn of 1993–94, when the Commission proposed a new priority objective — **Objective 6** — for regions with extremely low population density. Objective 1 required GDP per capita **below 75 per cent** of the EU average, which Finland's north and east did not meet. Protocol No. 6 instead granted regions with **eight inhabitants per square kilometre or fewer** support equivalent to Objective 1 — **on the basis of a permanent geographical characteristic rather than an economic indicator.**
>
> **That redefinition is the analytically central point**: EU regional support was extended from *measured economic backwardness* to *permanent sparsity* — and the geography of that criterion **closely matched Finland's traditional support areas.**
>
> One more fact, because it frames everything: in the October 1994 referendum, 57 per cent voted in favour — but **farmers voted against by more than ninety per cent**, the north voted well below the national average, and about **seventy per cent of Centre Party supporters opposed membership**. **The regions and constituencies most opposed to accession were, in large part, those that Objective 6 and northern aid were designed to compensate.** I therefore read Objective 6 as **a politically constructed entitlement**: at the moment of its creation, the complementarity of EU intervention was **already being interpreted in terms of a member state's domestic requirements.**

**日本語訳**：EU層がどう作られたかを手短に。その作られ方が、読み方を左右するからです。／2つのショックが同時に来ました。**1991年のソ連崩壊**が西側統合を安全保障上の優先課題にし、フィンランドは1992年3月に申請します。同時に**経済が急収縮**——対ソ貿易の消滅、信用ブームの終焉、1994年には失業率が**18%**を超えました。巨額の赤字を前に、国庫支出金の削減が政治日程に上がります。／決定的な障害は**農業**でした。農業者団体MTK——アホ首相の中央党の中核支持基盤——が強く反対し、1992年の報告書は、特別措置なしにCAPを受け入れれば**生産者価格は半減**、生産は約**80%減**と予測しました。**こうして農業・農村への補償が、加盟そのものの政治的条件になったのです。**／突破口は1993–94年の変わり目、欧州委員会が極端に人口密度の低い地域のための新目標**目標6**を提案したときに開かれました。目標1は1人当たりGDPがEU平均の**75%未満**を要求し、フィンランドの北部・東部は該当しません。議定書6は代わりに、**人口密度8人/km²以下**の地域に目標1相当の支援を与えました——**経済指標ではなく恒久的な地理的特性に基づいて**。／**この再定義が分析上の核心です**：EU地域支援は「計測される経済的後進性」から「恒久的な人口希薄性」へ拡張され、その基準の地理は**フィンランドの伝統的支援地域とほぼ一致しました**。／もう一つ、すべてを枠づける事実を。1994年10月の国民投票では57%が賛成しましたが、**農民は90%超が反対**、北部は全国平均を大きく下回り、**中央党支持者の約70%が加盟に反対**でした。**加盟に最も反対した地域と支持層こそ、目標6と北部支援が補償するよう設計された対象だったのです。** ゆえに私は目標6を**政治的に構築された受給権**として読みます。創設のその瞬間に、EU介入の補完性は**すでに加盟国の国内的必要という観点から解釈されていた**のです。

## §6. 歴史②：国内層の収縮と分断された行政　⏱ 2:00　🖼 Slide 7–8

🎯 **残す一文**: 「改革は代替を意図していないが、代替を実行しやすく観察しにくくした」／「反事実を誰も観察していなかった」

> Now the part the additionality literature tends not to look at: **what was happening to the domestic layer at the same moment.**
>
> Until the end of 1992, Finnish state grants were predominantly **task-specific and cost-based**: the state reimbursed a legally specified share of **actual costs**, with rates graduated by municipal fiscal capacity. That system had underwritten the post-war construction of the universal service state in the periphery — matching grants **lowered the effective price of expanding services**, and graduated rates channelled proportionally more to poor, sparsely populated municipalities.
>
> The reform of January 1993 replaced this with **prospective, criteria-based general grants**, calculated from population, age structure, morbidity and other need indicators **rather than actual costs**.
>
> **Two features matter for my argument.** First, the reform **decoupled state funding from actual service costs**: grants became **fungible** revenue from the municipality's point of view, and the state could adjust the total volume of grants **without legislating any change in service obligations**. Second, the reform was prepared and enacted **in the deepest trough of the fiscal crisis**, and one of its practical attractions for central government was precisely that **it made grant volumes controllable.**
>
> **That controllability was used immediately.** State grants were cut repeatedly from the mid-1990s, under both the Aho and the Lipponen governments. Municipalities absorbed the cuts through expenditure restraint, higher municipal income tax rates, higher user fees and reduced investment. And **the burden of adjustment fell disproportionately on municipalities with weak tax bases and unfavourable demographics — the small, remote municipalities of the north and east, which were simultaneously the designated beneficiaries of Objective 6.**
>
> This has two implications. **Additionality is assessed against a counterfactual baseline of national expenditure** — but where that baseline is **itself being cut for consolidation reasons**, EU co-financed activity can be presented as supplementary even if the aggregate flow of public resources to assisted regions stagnates or falls. And the 1993 reform **changed the interface**: under cost reimbursement, an external project grant would have interacted transparently with itemised state reimbursements; under general grants, state transfers, municipal tax revenue and EU co-financing met in **a single fungible budget**, and **no institution was tasked with tracing whether EU-funded activity displaced what domestic funding would otherwise have supported.**
>
> I want to put this carefully: **the reformed grant system was not designed to accommodate substitution — but it had the effect of making substitution easier to effect and harder to observe.**
>
> And the administration matched. Cohesion policy presupposes that a member state can receive the funds in a **cross-sectoral, integrated** manner: additionality requires **an actor able to observe total public expenditure across sectors.** In Finland, management of the Structural Funds was **distributed across the Ministry of the Interior, the Ministry of Labour, the Ministry of Agriculture and Forestry and others.** Each ministry executed EU funds as an **extension of its own budget lines**. Nobody had the mandate or the incentive to check **horizontally** whether EU subsidies were displacing domestic expenditure elsewhere in the state budget. Monitoring was so deficient in the early years that part of the European Social Fund allocation **could not be used**.
>
> **I do not claim this structure was designed to obstruct verification.** What the record shows is a long-standing sectoral administration meeting a new cross-sectoral requirement. **But its effect was that the counterfactual against which additionality would have to be judged was not observed by anyone.**

## §7. 実証①：配分と自治体レベルの規模　⏱ 1:30　🖼 Slide 9–10（表2・表3・表5）

🎯 **残す一文**: EU層は小さい。関心は量ではなく形式と標的にある

> To the evidence. First, programme level. Finland received **1,652 million ECU** for 1995–99, dispersed across five objectives — a pattern contrasting with the cohesion countries' concentration on Objective 1, and one that **increased the administrative workload** on both sides. The largest single share went to **Objective 6: 450 million, or 27 per cent** — substantially larger than Sweden's, in both absolute and relative terms.
>
> Within Objective 6, the most notable feature is that **nearly half — 46 per cent — went to agriculture, forestry, fisheries and rural development, under the Ministry of Agriculture and Forestry.** The funds thus operated **largely through the existing policy structure**. And the geography reinforces the point: the Objective 6 area **overlaps closely** with the new national **northern aid** scheme, whose zoning rests on a **traditional domestic indicator**. So the new framework, while formally novel, **reproduced much of the geography of the traditional support areas.**
>
> But — and this is the first sign of transformation — **more than half of Objective 6, 53.6 per cent, went to competitiveness-oriented fields**: business development and human resources. The allocation logic was **continuous in target and function, discontinuous in method**: resources now flowed through **competitive application procedures** rather than general transfers and price guarantees. **The centre — and now the EU — steered the periphery through competitions rather than through entitlement formulas.**
>
> Now the municipal ledger. From 1997, subsidies booked as received "from the European Union" appear: **96, 160 and 151 million markka** in 1997, '98 and '99 — **406.9 million in total**, received at some point by **315 of the 452 municipalities**.
>
> **The scale of this inflow disciplines every claim that follows.** Over three years, 406.9 million markka corresponds to roughly **six to ten per cent** of the state's narrow sectoral subsidies to municipalities in the same years, and to **a fraction of one per cent of total state grants.** **The EU layer was small relative to the municipal finance system as a whole. Whatever else it did, it did not finance the welfare state. Its interest lies in its form and its targeting, not its volume.**
>
> Two features of its incidence, both imprecisely estimated. **The probability of receiving EU funds was almost identical inside and outside the Objective 6 area** — a difference of 0.3 percentage points, with an interval of roughly plus or minus nine points. **There is no evidence of differential access.** And per-resident **amounts** were larger in Objective 6 municipalities in point-estimate terms — about **87 per cent** higher — **but the interval runs from minus two to plus two hundred and fifty-eight per cent and therefore includes no difference.** So I say amounts were **larger in point estimates**; I do **not** say they were **concentrated** there.
>
> What can be said with more confidence concerns **form** rather than location: receipts were **lumpy, project-shaped and unevenly distributed** — **the opposite of the smooth, formula-based general grants they historically accompanied.**

## §8. 実証②：機能的分業とゼロ結果　⏱ 2:30　🖼 Slide 11–12（表6・図3・図4）

🎯 **残す一文**: EU層は福祉ではなく開発を支えた／台帳での相殺の不在は代替の証拠ではない

> For welfare state analysis, **the functional composition is the most consequential result.**
>
> Aggregating the 52 accounting functions into harmonised groups: the largest receiving categories are **general administration and planning at 29 per cent**, **economic and business development at 20**, and **education and culture at 19**, the latter spread across 206 municipalities — the widest reach of any group. The administration aggregate must be read with care: **the top ten municipalities account for 81.5 per cent of it**, which suggests it operates partly as **an accounting entry point for projects** rather than as a final functional use.
>
> **Core welfare services — social welfare, health care and social services — received 37.6 million markka: 9.2 per cent of the total.** And within this group **the Objective 6 area was not favoured**: the point estimate implies per-resident receipts about **seventy per cent lower** than outside — a rate ratio of 0.27 — though that association **weakens under multiple-testing adjustment and should be treated as suggestive only.**
>
> **The mirror image is economic and business development**, where the Objective 6 association is **both the largest and the only one that survives adjustment**: a rate ratio of approximately **seven**, with an FDR-adjusted p of 0.048. **Even here the interval is very wide** — it runs from plus seventy to plus two thousand seven hundred per cent — so **the estimate establishes the sign of the association, not its magnitude.**
>
> I describe this as **a functional division of labour between the layers of territorial finance. The EU layer did not finance the welfare services of the periphery. The functions it financed were developmental — business development, projects, human capital — while services continued to be funded from the national layer: sectoral state subsidies, the reformed general grants and tax-based equalisation, all of which were simultaneously being cut and recalibrated.**
>
> Then the direct test. **Did EU money visibly replace state subsidies within the same municipality and function? At ledger level, the answer is no.** Classifying cells by the joint movement of EU-source and state sectoral subsidies: **genuine addition — EU up, state not down — accounts for 64.7 per cent of cells. Strict apparent complementarity — an EU increase approximately offsetting a state decrease — accounts for 1.3 per cent**, and for **none** of the 74 cells inside the Objective 6 area. Nor is the correlation negative, as crowding out would imply: it is approximately **plus 0.05**.
>
> There is **weak** evidence of what I call **functional maintenance**: among cells where EU funding rose while state subsidies fell, real expenditure was maintained or increased in **73.9 per cent** of Objective 6 cells against **57.4** outside — a difference of 16.5 points, **with an interval from minus 0.5 to plus 33.7, which includes zero.** Under a stricter definition the difference falls to under four points. **I therefore treat functional maintenance as a pattern worth further examination, not as an established difference.**
>
> **The negative result is as informative as the positive ones — but it must be interpreted carefully.** If pseudo-complementarity had operated as a **municipal-ledger swap** — EU money in, state subsidy out, line by line — the strict apparent-complementarity type would be **common**. It is **rare**. **What follows is that this mechanism is not supported by the data. It does not follow that substitution occurred elsewhere.** The absence of ledger-level offsetting is **equally consistent** with substitution in the state budget, which municipal accounts cannot observe, **and with no substitution having taken place.** Discriminating between those requires ministerial budget records and project-level payment data.

## §9. 結論　⏱ 1:30　🖼 Slide 13–14

🎯 **残す一文**: 3次元のうち2つが支持され、1つは確立されない。概念の貢献は分析的である

> Let me close.
>
> The answer has three parts. **The EU layer was constructed as institutionally complementary**: Objective 6 converted a domestic political requirement — compensating the constituencies whose consent accession required — into **a permanent geographical entitlement in EU law**. **The domestic layer was simultaneously restructured**: the 1993 shift to fungible general grants, consolidation-driven cuts and reformed equalisation **reduced and reshaped the baseline against which additionality would have had to be assessed** — in an administration where **no actor was positioned to observe that baseline across sectors.** And **in municipal accounts, the EU layer appears as a small, project-shaped, development-oriented inflow that did not visibly displace state subsidies line by line, while core welfare services received less than a tenth of it.**
>
> **So the concept earns a correspondingly limited theoretical claim. Two of its three dimensions are supported: institutional complementarity is documented, and administrative transformation is evident in both allocation records and municipal receipts. The third — fiscal substitution — is not established by this evidence.** The contribution of the concept is therefore **analytical rather than evidentiary**: it separates dimensions that are routinely conflated in debates about additionality, and it **specifies what each would require to be shown.**
>
> Read through Brenner, the episode is better described as **rescaling than as retrenchment**: the resources sustaining peripheral provision were not simply reduced — **their composition, conditionality and spatial logic were reorganised**, with the criterion of entitlement shifted from service cost and fiscal capacity **to permanent geographical sparsity**. Governance became **multilevel in form while remaining centrally steered in substance.**
>
> The generalisable proposition, stated cautiously: **in service-based welfare states with decentralised delivery, external transfers interact with the domestic grant and equalisation system in ways that can leave the aggregate fiscal effect indeterminate at the level where the transfer is recorded, while changing the governance form of territorial finance in ways that are directly observable.**
>
> And that has a live implication. **Assessments of cohesion policy — and, prospectively, of instruments such as the Recovery and Resilience Facility — that ignore the domestic intergovernmental baseline risk misreading both their distributive and their institutional effects.**
>
> Progress from here requires sources this paper does not use: **ministerial budget records and project-level payment data** would let the upstream substitution hypothesis be tested directly, and **comparison with Sweden — same protocol, smaller allocation, different domestic grant regime** — would show how far the configuration depends on the Finnish setting.
>
> Thank you. I look forward to your comments.

**日本語訳**：結びます。／答えは3部構成です。**EU層は制度的に補完的に構築されました**。目標6は、加盟の同意が必要だった支持層を補償するという国内の政治的要請を、**EU法上の恒久的な地理的受給権**へ転換しました。**国内層は同時に再構築されていました**。1993年の代替可能な一般財源型への転換、緊縮による削減、改革された財政調整が、**追加性が判定されるべきベースラインを縮小し作り変え**——しかもその行政では、**部門を横断してそのベースラインを観察できる主体がいませんでした。** そして**自治体会計におけるEU層は、小さく、プロジェクト形状で、開発志向の流入として現れ、国庫補助金を項目ごとに目に見える形で押し出してはおらず、中核的福祉サービスが受け取ったのはその1割未満でした。**／**したがって概念が得る理論的請求権も、それに見合って限定的です。3次元のうち2つが支持されます：制度的補完性は文書化され、行政的変容は配分記録と自治体の受取の双方で明白です。第三の財政的代替は、この証拠では確立されません。** 概念の貢献は**証拠的というより分析的**です——追加性論争でルーティンに混同される諸次元を分離し、**各次元を示すには何が必要かを特定する**のです。／ブレナーを通して読めば、このエピソードは**削減というよりリスケーリング**として記述する方が適切です。周辺部の供給を支える資源は単に減らされたのではなく、**その構成・条件性・空間的論理が再編され**、受給要件はサービス費用と財政力から**恒久的な地理的希薄性**へ移りました。ガバナンスは**形式において多層的になりながら、実質において中央操舵のまま**でした。／一般化可能な命題を慎重に述べれば：**分権的供給をもつサービス型福祉国家において、外部移転は国内の補助金・財政調整制度と、移転が記録される水準では集計的財政効果を不確定なままにしうる一方、領域財政のガバナンス形式は直接観察可能な仕方で変える、という形で相互作用する。**／これには現在進行形の含意があります。**国内の政府間ベースラインを無視した結束政策の評価は——そして今後の復興・レジリエンス・ファシリティのような手段の評価は——その分配的効果と制度的効果の双方を読み違えるリスクを負います。**／ここから先に進むには本稿が使っていない資料が要ります。**省庁の予算記録とプロジェクト段階の支払データ**があれば上流代替仮説を直接検証でき、**スウェーデンとの比較——同じ議定書、より小さい配分、異なる国内補助金レジーム**——は、この配置がどこまでフィンランド固有の環境に依存するかを示すでしょう。／ありがとうございました。コメントを楽しみにしています。

## 話し方のメモ

- **数字は概数で言う**：「1,506.7マルッカ」ではなく "roughly fifteen hundred markka per resident"。精密さは論文が担保する。口頭では聞き取りやすさを優先。
- **限界は謝らない**：*"Several key estimates are imprecise, and I report them as such throughout."* と平板に述べる。声を落とさない。これは設計であって弁明ではない。
- **3つのキーフレーズは間を取って言う**：
  1. "Universalism is a territorial achievement."
  2. "It made substitution easier to effect and harder to observe."
  3. "The counterfactual was not observed by anyone."
- **表を映すときは数字を読み上げない**：「注目していただきたいのは1行だけです」と言って1つだけ指す。
- **ストリーム25の他報告への接続**：分権と近接性（Oliveira et al.）、地域格差の地図化（Göbel et al.）、多層ガバナンスの実施（Le Cozannet-Laidin）、地域の知識経済とポピュリスト投票（Im）。特に最後は本稿の "places that don't matter" の議論と直結するので、質疑で **"That connects directly to Zhen Jie Im's paper in this session"** のように橋を架けると議論が伸びる。
